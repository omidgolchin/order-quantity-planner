How the verdict is decided rules, formulas, sources

The order quantity is the newsvendor solution (Arrow, Harris & Marschak 1951): order up to the demand quantile set by your economics, not to the forecast. One unit too many loses (cost − salvage); one unit too few loses (price − cost + goodwill). The balance point is the critical ratio, and the tool orders the smallest quantity whose chance of covering demand reaches it.

critical ratio = (price − cost + goodwill) / (price − salvage + goodwill) order = smallest Q with P(demand ≤ Q) ≥ critical ratio

The forecast is chosen by backtest, not by fit. Seasonal naive, ETS and ARIMA forecast the last 8 periods one step at a time, each time using only the past (rolling origin; Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, Forecasting: Principles and Practice, 3rd ed.). The lowest error wins, and its 80% interval coverage is shown: a method can win on error and still be overconfident. (In your browser, a pasted series races seasonal naive against ETS; the built-in products were also raced against ARIMA offline, by the Python reference implementation in this repository.)

Sold-out periods hide demand. If a period sold exactly the stock on hand, demand was at least that high. With a stock column, those periods are replaced by draws from the sales seen at or above that level before anything is fit (a Kaplan–Meier style fix), which widens the top of the demand range instead of understating it.

Intermittent demand (30% or more of periods at zero) breaks ETS and ARIMA assumptions, so the tool samples past periods directly and says so (Syntetos & Boylan 2005).

How last period is graded what counts as money left on the table

Your realized profit is computed from what actually sold. The plan's order is the recommendation from Plan an order for the same product and prices. "Left on the table" compares expected profit (over the whole demand distribution) at the plan's quantity against your quantity: one period is a single draw, so a good order can still lose money and a bad one can get lucky. If you sold out, your sales are a floor on demand, not demand itself: the grade says so instead of treating the period as fully observed.
